Distortion as acceptance - codebook

This codebook was developed to ensure transparency and replicability of the qualitative analysis. It contains definitions, inclusion criteria and examples for each category, and is organized into three sections reflecting the three analyses conducted: experiential shifts, metaphor and affective responses. Codes were applied to the data using Taguette.

General coding rules

  • A single fragment may be coded under multiple categories across the three analyses ( experiential shifts, metaphor & affective responses) simultaneously.
  • Highly conventionalised spatial terms for pitch such as 'high' and 'low' were not coded as spatial metaphors, as these terms are directly adopted from the interface and do not reflect spontaneous metaphorical language use by the participant.

1: Experiential shifts

This analysis encompasses shifts in perspective, intensity and perceived realness of the thought as reported by participants during interaction with the interface.

Increased intensity categorizes phrases and words that display an increase of intensity experienced during the experiment. The phrases that are used to describe these are descriptive. Affective states are excluded from this categorisation and will be coded separately. This way we can code whether or not there was an increase or decrease of intensity during the experience, but in parallel also code which affective states are present. To illustrate this, an example response will be coded that includes an affective state and an increase in intensity. Only the section that describes the increased intensity is coded between [brackets].

For example,: ' De modulatie van reverb zorgde bij mij voor het meeste ongemak in mijn beleving [omdat het mijn zin versterkte.] Het gaf een heel eenzaam gevoel.' (The reverb modulation caused me the most discomfort in my experience [because it strengthened my sentence.] It gave a very lonely feeling.).

Decreased intensity categorizes phrases and words that display a decrease of intensity experienced during the experiment. The phrases that are used to describe these are descriptive. Affective states are excluded from this categorisation and will be coded separately. This way we can code whether or not there was an increase or decrease of intensity during the experience, but in parallel also code which affective states are present. To illustrate this, an example response will be shown that includes a decrease in intensity.

For example: 'Het combineren van 2 modulaties zorgde ervoor dat mijn beleving afnam.' (combining two modulations caused my experience to diminish)

Outsider and insider perspective refer to the two viewpoints from which participants may experience the thought. Perspective shifts are considered relevant to cognitive defusion, as the ability to observe thoughts from an external viewpoint is a core component of defusion within the ACT framework [3][11]. Both perspectives are coded because research suggests that the dynamic alternation between them, rather than a linear shift from one to the other, is associated with greater flexibility and adaptive change [38]. Coding both perspectives allows for an assessment of whether the modulations produced a shift in perspective, and in which direction.

Outsider perspective is categorized as adopting the position of an observer rather than the author of the thought. Within the ACT framework, this is described as stepping back from the meaning of verbal processes and witnessing them from the point of view of an observer, consistent with the concept of self-as-context [3][11]. Self-distancing research additionally documents this shift, showing that observing oneself from an external viewpoint reduces the intensity and duration of emotional responses [33][35]. Drawing on both frameworks allows for a broader operationalization of the category, capturing any instance in which the participant adopted an external perspective toward the thought, regardless of the underlying mechanism.

For example: 'het leek alsof ik naar andere personen aan het luisteren was' (it felt as if I was listening to other people); 'niet meer mezelf die ik hoorde' (no longer myself that I heard).

Insider perspective is categorized as the thought was experienced as one's own, from a first-person viewpoint. Although ACT describes cognitive fusion as treating thoughts as literal truths rather than mental events, it does not explicitly operationalize the perspective from which this occurs and therefore cannot be coded as such. This category is nonetheless included for the reasons outlined in the introduction to this section. Self-immersion research however, describes this as experiencing events through one's own eyes, with an absence of psychological distance [37].

For example: 'alleen was ik het zelf' (only it was me myself); 'it felt like I actually said it during a moment of sadness'.

Deliteralization refers to instances where the sentence felt less meaningful or less real. This is consistent with ACT's description of deliteralization as a temporary loss or weakening of word meaning as a core component of cognitive defusion [3][11].

For example: 'het werd meer een geluid dan een zin, waardoor het bijna geen betekenis meer had' (it became more of a sound than a sentence, causing it to lose almost all meaning); 'it did not feel real'.

2: Metaphor

The metaphor analysis assesses the language participants spontaneously used to describe their experience of the experiment. A metaphor is understood here as an expression in which language from one domain is used to describe an experience in another [39]. Some terms are theoretically metaphorical in origin(such as 'high/low' for pitch), but as participants adopted these directly from the interface rather than generating them independently, they were not coded. The following categories were identified inductively from the data.

Spatial is categorized as metaphors of location or distance, describing where the thought was perceived to be. This includes descriptions of the thought as positioned in a specific place or moving closer or further away. Coded when the fragment explicitly describes location or distance, not when it describes a personification, or sound texture. To illustrate this, an example response will be coded that includes a personification and a spatial metaphor. Only the spatial metaphor is coded between [brackets].

For example: 'dit gecombineerd met reverb op hoge stand zorgde ervoor dat klonk alsof iemand [in een andere ruimte] dit aan het zeggen was tegen iemand anders' (this combined with reverb on a high setting caused it to sound as if someone [in another room] was saying this to someone else).

Personification is a metaphor where the participant describes the modulated audio as though it were spoken or produced by a person, entity or separate agent with its own character, voice or intention. This differs from outsider perspective, which describes a shift in the position of the participant as observer. For example: 'dit zorgde ervoor dat het leek alsof ik naar andere personen aan het luisteren was' (This caused it to feel as if I was listening to other people). Personification instead refers to a shift in how the modulated audio itself is characterized namely, as having a separate identity regardless of the participant's own perspective.

For example: 'een nare priester die tegen mij aan het opdreunen was' (a sinister priest reciting at me); 'een stem in mijn hoofd die tegen mij praatte' (a voice in my head talking to me).

Weight is a metaphor of physical weight(heavy or light) applied to the experience of the sentence. The intensity categories however, describe an increase or decrease in how strongly the sentence was experienced. For example: 'omdat het mijn zin versterkte' ( because it intensified my sentence) ; 'made me forget or alleviate the thought'. Weight on the other hand refers specifically to the use of physical weight as a metaphor.

For example: 'het gaf gewicht aan de zin' (it gave weight to the sentence).

Atmospheric are instances where the modulations evoked a particular mood or atmosphere.

For example: 'made the thought feel more ominous'; 'surreal yet more haunting'.

Sound texture metaphors describe the perceived quality of the modulations.

For example: 'de zin werd meer vervaagd' (the sentence became more blurred); 'de zin voelde meer als geluid, een deksel ofzo' (the sentence felt more like a sound, like a lid).

Repetition are metaphors in which the looping of the audio is described as a recurring or cyclical experience.

For example: 'omdat het als een negatief [mantra] ging voelen' (because it started to feel like a negative mantra); 'made the sentence feel like a [repeating mantra] during a dream or nightmare'.

3: Affective states

Affective states responses were coded based on emotional states explicitly reported by participants, as well as affective states inferred by the researcher from the language used[32]. Below the list of all affective states that were coded.

Humour defined as words and/or phrases expressing amusement or finding the modulation funny.

For example: 'ik moest lachen' (I had to laugh); 'dit vond ik vrij humoristisch' (I found this quite humorous).

Discomfort defined as words and/or phrases expressing a general sense of unease or discomfort in response to the modulation.

For example: 'meer confronterend en beladen gevoel, ongemak' (more confronting and heavy feeling, discomfort).

Hurt defined as words and/or phrases expressing a sense of pain or being hurt in response to the manipulation.

For example: 'more hurtful'.

Loneliness defined as words and/or phrases expressing a sense of loneliness or isolation in response to the modulation.

For example: 'het gaf een heel eenzaam gevoel' (it gave a very lonely feeling).

Pleasantness defined as words and/or phrases expressing a general sense of agreeableness or positive feeling in response to the modulation.

For example: 'veel prettiger' (much more pleasant); 'het positiever werd' (it became more positive).

Sadness defined as words and/or phrases expressing a sense of sadness in response to the modulation.

For example: 'during a moment of sadness'.

Seriousness defined as words and/or phrases expressing that the modulation made the sentence feel more serious.

For example: 'it seemed more serious'.

Self-compassion defined as words and/or phrases expressing compassion or sympathy toward oneself in response to the modulation.

For example: 'mogelijk voelde ik daardoor wat meer zelfmedelijden dat ik dit over mezelf denk' (possibly I felt more self-compassion that I think this about myself).
